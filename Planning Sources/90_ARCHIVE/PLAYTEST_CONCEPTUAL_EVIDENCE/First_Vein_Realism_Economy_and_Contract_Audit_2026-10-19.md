## Executive audit verdict
1. Wages ($3.00 inexperienced, $4.25 skilled, $4.50 lead) are broadly plausible for a 1877 Black Hills camp. An 1878 published travel account says experienced miners commonly earned $4–$5/day and beginners substantially less. Six-worker actual payroll of **$21/day** and **$126 per six-day week** is mathematically correct. An experienced manager might command more, depending on conditions and authority.
2. Historical rock movement in THE FIRST VEIN at ~0.20–0.30 short ton **total excavated rock per miner-day** is physically believable for manual, selective, difficult work, but **commercial ore yield** at ~25–30% of output makes the enterprise almost inevitably lose money at these wages and previous buyer prices. It is not a plausible *sustainably profitable* equilibrium for the user's intended scenario.
3. Previous completed buyer payments ($95.25 for 23,284 lb = **$8.18/short ton averaged**) are harsh for proven richer lots. The last 9,592 lb sale realized $39.50 ($8.24/ton). Q005/Q006 sample assays (~0.31 and 2.40 oz/ton) imply about 5.668 oz contained in the pre-sample 4.8 t (value **$117.16** at $20.67/oz). Selling for $39.50 is ~34% of assay-implied metal value. Low bids can exist, but making every buyer bid so poorly makes the market implausibly hostile as a *default*. Historic contemporaneous reports distinguish reliable commercial mill yields (~$15–$20/ton in the paying claims) from high-grade picked pieces and say most claims did not pay.
4. A single mule wagon with conservative **1,000 lb** payload cannot haul a proposed **10 short tons/week** to Deadwood: minimum **20 loaded wagon trips**, ignoring return, loading, weather and conflicts with ordinary freight. It can still save smaller real freight/supply expenses when unused capacity is available; ownership does not erase labor, animal upkeep, wear or opportunity costs.
5. The quoted ~8-foot arrastra at **0.25–0.50 ton/workday**, animal/worker operating **$5.90/workday** is an illustrative planning option, not a verified local historical transaction. An approximately ten-foot traditional arrastra is documented at about **one ton per 24 hours**, not a ton every 8-hour shift. It cannot process a profitable high-volume six-miner mine's full feed. Retain $238–$260 as fictional provisional quotes until better period evidence is found; exclude land, rights, overrun, water, tailings and working capital.
6. Gold price of ~$20.67 per troy oz is period-correct for US gold accounting. Do not count contained gold, assayed gold, raw ore sales or an estimated 0.0044 oz personal recovered sample as **recovered-and-sold** ounces. The latter gold is unsold, its mass provisional.

## Productivity: honest target, not a magical profit multiplier
**Observed**: six miners worked 6 days yielding 7.05 short tons (~0.196 t per worker-day), next 6 days 10.50 tons (~0.292 t per worker-day), and 11 days 18.70 tons (~0.283 t per worker-day). Selected mineral-bearing rock has been only around 25–30% of all material moved. These figures could represent difficult hand-pick development, stabilization and selective work, **not** mature commercial high-output operations.

**Authoring hypothesis for a viable later workface (not yet established in-world):** 14–22 total short tons excavated/week using six miners with relatively easy surface access; 8–12 short tons of *payable ore* only where the actual geological truth supports that high fraction. This is an illustrative scenario-generation range, **not** a historical universal productivity rate, not a confirmed discovery, and not authorization for explosives, machinery or hazardous excavation. Less favorable conditions should produce losses and managerial adaptation.

### Illustrative six-day commercial week, grade verified, full crew paid
Assume 10 short tons of legitimately payable ore, buyer collection for the bulk (our wagon handles small support/courier movements), and a negotiated net price of **$17.50 per ton** after buyer's processing/hauling deductions. The ore's underlying grade and recovery must realistically support the buyer price; e.g. hypothetical ~1.4 oz/ton assayed grade and ~80% buyer recovery yield $23.15/ton metal revenue, leaving ~$5.65/ton for buyer treatment, haulage, risk and profit.

|---|---:|---:|
| Payment for 10 t ore | +$175.00 | +$175.00 |
| Six miners, six paid days | -$126.00 | -$126.00 |
| Mine timber/tools/consumables | -$16.00 | -$16.00 |
| Sampling, assay, local overhead | -$9.00 | -$9.00 |
| Mine stockpile/handling incidentals | -$7.00 | -$7.00 |
| **Incremental small freight/courier service cost** | **-$5.00** | **-$15.00** |
| **Indicative operating contribution** | **+$12.00 (6.9%)** | **+$2.00 (1.1%)** |

**Cautions:** These are prospective *calibration target* prices/costs, not offers/contracts, and do not count the wagon's historical cost, secured-note interest, depreciation, major repair, owner time, land/mill debt service, taxes or major development. A fully costed mine profit margin can be lower or negative. The sample's own-wagon advantage is **$10/week** because it substitutes for a small amount of contracted support freight, **not** because one wagon transports ten tons every week. Buyer handles the bulk shipment with larger capacity. Do not book $175 or a profit in the simulation until there is actual ore and completed sale.

A materially lower grade, 5 rather than 10 payable tons, or repeated $8/ton buyer offers would invalidate the example and make the mine lose money. The game should allow that result: 1877 reports say only a minority of Black Hills claims were paying. **Scenario design choice**: for THE FIRST VEIN, generate or validate at least one plausible viable ore zone reachable through player investment, rather than making profitable output a universal magic target.

### Required retunes
- Distinguish `rock excavated`, `payable ore`, `mineral contained (assay-estimated)`, `actual recovered metal`, `paid cash`, `true net mine profit`. Waste is normally not saleable. Mixed rock can have a buyer if net recoverable content and logistics support it; no automatic market for all material.
- Sellable lots get credible quotes based on independent samples/recovery risk/size/transport; buyer's net value cannot systematically remain 25–35% of contained-value unless there is credible metallurgy and freight explanation.
- Add grade-to-value reconciliation and assay uncertainty. Introduce large-sample trial milling to estimate recovery when warranted.
- Productivity must come from face accessibility, grades, equipment, shift duration, grade control, handling distance, geology and people, not a flat global `mining × 2` boost. No new discovery is known yet.
- Amos automatically schedules six-day operations and batches raw-ore sale settlement under the user's recent delegation, up to solvency/safety/credit boundaries. Pause only for financing, hiring, major capex, serious legal or safety exceptions. Accurate six-week cash burn matters more than many repeated procurement choices.
- Seller-financed land/equipment requires separate title, security and debt truth. A lease is not fee-simple ownership; patent rights cannot be inferred from a land-office entry. Validate legal period and geography with qualified sources.
- Retain actual historical playtest receipts, ore weights, wages and payroll as **completed simulated events**. No cash or ore creation as a tuning adjustment. Correction only to December accrued interest read models. Future scenario generator should implement the target calibration.

## Further accounting and cost realism holds
- **Mine economic entity:** Most historic checkpoint mine wages/receipts run through the *owner personal cash account* rather than a separately capitalized mine Business. That is valid as an individual proprietorship, but the future profitable-mine objective needs an **explicit mine operating-profit account/read model**, separating gross receipts, extraction labor, transport, treatment, ongoing repairs, depreciation, working-capital advances and financing interest per intended scenario definition. Owner cash is not mine profit.
- **Missing household subsistence:** The simulation's earlier unidentified personal-living-expense gap is a real QA blocker. Do not declare full balance-sheet equity audited or make lifestyle costs vanish. This gap remains open in the audit addendum.
- **Freight overstated apparent returns:** Eli's $105 receipts minus $42 wages, $32 feed and $9 supplies = $22 period cash operating surplus, **not** net profit. Include wagon/animal depreciation, shoeing, accidents, stable dues, secured chattel interest and any arm's-length equipment-use rent between owner and freight firm. Internal freight charges cancel on consolidation, while physical movement costs stay real.
- **Land $210 and wagon $365:** Quotes are fictional. A $210 small peripheral parcel and $365 used wagon/two mules are not automatically absurd, but there is not yet contemporaneous comparable evidence for condition, acreage, location, title or full tack scope. Treat as locally quoted prices, **historical plausibility unverified**; no universal pricing table should be locked from this example.
- **Equipment $238/$260 and $18 custom mill minimum:** Likewise fictional plausible-looking planning quotes; no primary local invoice verified. Preserve if the NPC offers stand, but mark calibration provisional. Small arrastra throughput and operating costs create a real bottleneck even if price seems affordable.
- **Seasonality, six-day labor:** Winter can interrupt wagon routes, daylight and prospecting. Workers can leave if many weeks pass without shifts. The manager must allocate real paid days, not use unpaid indefinitely retained workers as a free standby crew.
- **Cash reserve:** Protecting the Jan 26 wagon balloon is a user/manager choice, not a separate restricted bank account. Financing/refinancing can preserve operations, but cannot itself create operating profit.

## Lease improvements / buyout rights — design note
**When landlord sells the land:** A valid lease generally does not vanish solely from a fee owner's sale; successor obligations depend on enforceability, priorities and whether buyer took subject to the lease (including notice/recording). An outright purchaser of the land is not automatically entitled to expel a valid protected tenant. But tenant default, a valid termination clause, competing superior rights or foreclosure can create risks. No universal guarantee of tenants' protection across every 1870s jurisdiction.

**Who owns a $200 building installed by tenant?** Depends on nature of attachment, trade-fixture rules, lease wording, intention, local law, timing of removal and the actual owner of the equipment vs underlying land. A lessee does **not** automatically get an unconditional $200 refund. Trade fixtures may be removable during the tenancy under historically established doctrine (U.S. Supreme Court, *Van Ness v. Pacard* 1829), while many permanent improvements can become part of real property; an 1884 mining-case example (*Conrad v. Saginaw Mining Co.*) protected removal of industrial equipment/buildings as trade fixtures in its specific context.

**Negotiable contract clauses, using generic agreement mechanics:**
- Fixed-duration lease, assignability, survival upon land sale, non-disturbance/recognition from mortgagee and successors as appropriate.
- **Tenant right of first refusal** or separate **option to purchase** the land at specified price or independent appraisal.
- Tenant removal rights for mill equipment/structures at expiration, with restoration obligations, notice deadlines and damage costs.
- **Early-termination compensation** for documented permanent improvements: e.g. agreed straight-line 5-year recovery of $200, landlord's elective termination after 2 full years -> $120 remaining contractual buyout. This is **an illustrative negotiated contract formula**, not a statutory or historical default, and can be less/more than market value.
- Landlord purchase of tenant improvements based on agreed depreciated cost vs fair market appraisal vs negotiated buyout; distinguish refundable improvements from removable equipment.
- Proof of building cost via invoices, wages, materials, photographs where appropriate to date, witness receipts and contemporaneous ledgers.
- Sale and bankruptcy/foreclosure should transfer property rights, encumbrances and leases through actual legal processes, not reset them.

The player's preference to **buy fee title via seller-held mortgage** remains sensible for a site with substantial immovable machinery but does not eliminate default/foreclosure risk. The option to improve leased land remains a meaningful alternative for smaller entrepreneurs.

## Oral vs written contracts — evidence-centric system
- Oral agreement can be **binding** and be breached; written agreements can also be breached. Signature increases evidentiary clarity; it is not a compliance force field. *Grafton v. Cummings* (1878) and *Williams v. Morris* (1877) show 19th-century statute-of-frauds limits for contracts dealing with land, with locality-specific exceptions.
- Contracts needing legal formality by place/date/type cannot be freely enforced merely by saying them. Long leases, land sales, surety guarantees, certain high-value goods deals and other categories need context-specific validation. Oral promise and signed memorandum are different evidence states.
- Four convenient presentation modes: `Handshake`, `Written memorandum`, `Signed instrument`, `Recorded/notarized instrument`, all backed by the same **actual Agreement** truth with distinct evidentiary strength/legal prerequisites, and counterparty knowledge and potential forged papers.
- **Loss of the document does not automatically void the underlying agreement**. A contemporary Supreme Court case, *Tayloe v. Riggs* (1828), allowed proof that an original had been lost and addressed secondary evidence of its contents. Parties may possess their own copies, receipts, letters, witnesses or a public-record copy. Lost-document outcome should depend on surviving evidence and legal forum, not delete world agreement state.
- Business bookkeeping should preserve signed original location, duplicates, receipts, delivery evidence, dates, signatures, witness identities, terms, authority/agent powers, and physical risks (fire, water, theft, misfiling, fraudulent alteration). If a record is lost, evidentiary confidence and collection/litigation costs change.
- An NPC can lie, default, deny conversation, suppress a record or flee; the player can do the same. Existing independent evidence determines discovery/enforceability and legal consequences. Do not grant NPCs omniscient knowledge or let missing paper magic-away delivered goods/debt.
- An oral $800 lowball to buy a wheelwright business can be declined or later used as an anchoring memory; it does not magically convey title or satisfy statute-of-frauds formalities.
