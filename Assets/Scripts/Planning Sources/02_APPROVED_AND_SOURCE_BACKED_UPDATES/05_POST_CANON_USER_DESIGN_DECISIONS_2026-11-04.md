## Part 1 — Overall design philosophy

### 1.1 — Realism and historical plausibility

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Ask what people in this period with this information, incentives and material constraints would actually do; favor causation over scripted gamification and research dated historical claims.  
**Limits and examples:** Philosophical principle, not a numeric rule.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 1–3, 114, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 1–4, 88.

### 1.2 — Independent world evolution

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** People, business ownership, employees, property uses, leases, debt, buildings and opportunities must change through real processes over decades; startup data never resets live state.  
**Limits and examples:** No immutable opening-town assignment.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 1–3, 114, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 1–4, 88.

### 1.3 — Player-NPC economic parity

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** NPCs share the player’s physical, economic, property, contract, labor, lending, investing and business-formation authorities, subject to their actual knowledge, resources and judgement.  
**Limits and examples:** No player-only entrepreneurship or investment privileges.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 1–3, 114, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 1–4, 88.

### 1.4 — No scripted success or protection

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Do not protect a business, impose market shares, or predetermine economic winners to satisfy a scenario; actors survive, adapt or fail through actual economics.  
**Limits and examples:** Exceptions for bounded development fixtures cannot become world rules.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 1–3, 114, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 1–4, 88.

### 1.5 — Meaningful choices

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Offer distinct interactions only if they meaningfully differ in money, obligations, rights, risk, access, time, relationships or legality; avoid decorative option overload and verbose explanations.  
**Limits and examples:** Function and consequence precede cosmetic UI.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 1–3, 114, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 1–4, 88.


## Part 2 — Business failure and financial distress

### 2.1 — Permanent NPC business failure

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Any firm may close permanently when no viable lawful economic path remains; being the only local supplier is not immunity.  
**Limits and examples:** A bakery may fail and create unmet demand for another entrant.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.2 — Gradual financial distress

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Financial difficulties unfold through cash strain, attempts to adapt, renegotiation, borrowing, layoffs, equipment or real-estate sales, restructuring and possibly shutdown rather than immediate automatic deletion.  
**Limits and examples:** Choice depends on owner judgement and funds; these are possible responses, not mandatory stages.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.3 — Borrowing into distress

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Some people double down and borrow against hoped-for recovery while others liquidate early; flawed judgement and distinct personalities affect the path until lenders refuse.  
**Limits and examples:** Loans remain real liabilities; no unlimited credit.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.4 — Independent lender underwriting

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Creditors judge borrower, collateral, income, obligations and credit risk using their legitimate evidence and legal authority; refusal, default and enforcement have causal consequences.  
**Limits and examples:** No invisible debt reset.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.5 — Distress asset liquidation

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** An owner can sell actual transferable inventory, horses, wagons, equipment, buildings, land or business interests to raise cash, potentially at distressed prices; liens, leases and other rights survive.  
**Limits and examples:** Sell only what the principal owns or may transfer.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.6 — Distressed business acquisitions

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** A struggling owner can approach known people for a loan, new investment, or purchase discussion, and buyers can investigate commercial viability, assets, relationships and vertical integration without scripted offers.  
**Limits and examples:** Glen and Bakery are illustrative actors, not mandated outcomes.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.7 — Business due diligence and imperfect records

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Prospective buyers can seek books, cash, revenues, expenses, inventory, equipment, debt, obligations, tenure, contracts and prospects through lawful access; information quality, incomplete books and access limits affect confidence and terms.  
**Limits and examples:** No omniscient private-business finances.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.8 — Seller financing

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** A legitimate sale may combine cash with seller-carried promissory notes, interest, schedules, collateral and default remedies; record real lender and borrower rights.  
**Limits and examples:** Seven percent for three years is illustrative only.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.9 — Person persists after business failure

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Failure or dispossession must not delete the former owner or erase acquired skills, relationships, needs and options; they can seek work, change field, move or found another firm.  
**Limits and examples:** Bakery occupation example is not a fixed character path.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.

### 2.10 — Flight and extreme distress

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CLARIFIED / RESEARCH HOLD  
**Decision / retained scope:** Severe distress may cause flight from creditors or deprivation, including food insecurity; disappearance does not cancel debt, title, liens, custodial obligations or jurisdictional enforcement.  
**Limits and examples:** Exact historical flight, enforcement, food insecurity and mortality treatment needs research; no scripted starvation.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 15–16, 30, 105, 242; TECH-XIV pp. 9–13, 20, 26–28.


## Part 3 — NPC entrepreneurship

### 3.1 — NPC business formation

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** A Person with viable resources, opportunities, knowledge and access may establish a generic enterprise, lease space, acquire stock and hire labor.  
**Limits and examples:** Shane and $300 saloon start are examples, not startup thresholds.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 2–3, 7–8, 105, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 2, 4, 88, 158.

### 3.2 — Opportunity discovered from evidence

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / REAFFIRMED  
**Decision / retained scope:** NPCs notice actual shortages, prices, customer travel, weak service, unused assets, rentable space, labor supply, trade and cash opportunities; firms do not spawn from an arbitrary timer.  
**Limits and examples:** Opportunity is a read model over economic state.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 2–3, 7–8, 105, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 2, 4, 88, 158.

### 3.3 — Skill changes outcomes, not generic access permissions

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** CONFLICT RESOLVED BY SCOPE  
**Decision / retained scope:** A blacksmith needs substantial skill for safe, timely high-quality output; an untrained operator cannot instantly produce masterwork, but Canon XXXI still permits physically feasible ordinary attempts; enterprise owners may instead hire skilled workers.  
**Limits and examples:** Do NOT invent a universal BlacksmithClass hard gate. Specialist methods and law may impose separate physical or legal prerequisites.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 2–3, 7–8, 105, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 2, 4, 88, 158.

### 3.4 — Periodic economic evaluation

**Classification:** CONFIRMED USER DECISION  
**Reconciliation:** ALREADY COVERED / CLARIFIED  
**Decision / retained scope:** Persons, households and firms periodically consider cash, debt, essential needs, employment, consumption, savings, expansion, asset investment and business creation through their proper authorities.  
**Limits and examples:** No parallel shadow wallet or fictional balance.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 2–3, 7–8, 105, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 2, 4, 88, 158.

### 3.5 — Rename vague pressure label

**Classification:** USER DESIGN PREFERENCE  
**Reconciliation:** OPEN NAMING REVIEW  
**Decision / retained scope:** Avoid the generic label Pressure for user-facing or architectural concepts when a precise name such as unmet demand, cash strain, service gap, scarcity or opportunity is available.  
**Limits and examples:** Do not blindly rename all code uses or remove the real economic causes.  
**Related source routes:** CAN-XXXI pp. 2–3, 7–8, 105, 194; TECH-XIV pp. 2, 4, 88, 158.
