# LAND & LEDGERS
## HISTORICAL ECONOMY, PRICES AND MATERIALS CALIBRATION
### EVIDENCE LEDGER — CURRENT REFERENCE

**Date:** 8 October 2026 | **Area:** Dakota Territory / South Dakota and explicitly marked comparable period evidence | **Status:** Seed reference, not exhaustive price database.

## Research discipline

This document makes **no unsupported universal wage, rent, commodity price or cost claim**. The source material includes dated, physical examples useful for calibrating scale but too narrow to become full market price tables. Product prices should be generated through actual supplier offers, quality/grade, credit, delivered cost, bargaining, customer willingness and local conditions; period observations guide plausible ranges.

A citation must include its date, geographic market, currency/measurement unit, grade or specification, retail/wholesale status, cash/credit terms and any freight/packing included. Treat an advertising claim, estimate or lawsuit amount differently from completed sale accounts. Convert historical units explicitly (board foot vs thousand board feet, bushel vs hundredweight, live vs dressed weight).

## Evidence classes

- **Direct transaction/contract:** Dated recorded payment or contract with scope and counterparties.
- **Dated claim:** Newspaper advertisement, promotional prospectus or testimony: potentially useful but not a completed sale.
- **Period method analogue:** Physical production rate or equipment specification from contemporaneous manuals.
- **Historical synthesis/calibration:** Narrow suggested engineering/economic envelope derived from several anchors.
- **Research hold:** Price/rate missing direct historical backing; do not invent a value because a game system needs one.

## Implementation boundaries

1. Preserve exact authoring currency, unit, and contract terms; a dollar in a claim document is not a separately stored modern purchasing-power score.
2. Landed cost should distinguish invoice cost, actual freight, handling, deposits, tax/duty where applicable, spoilage and financed carrying costs.
3. Prices can change because supply, demand, credit, weather, quality and routes change; no historical month-based price buff is justified without underlying physical changes.
4. Worker time and crew composition determine payroll and output. A benchmark rate is never added directly to money.
5. A scenario-specific example or autonomous playtest-generated cash total does not become authoritative economic calibration.
6. Historical retail, supplier and livestock records should be stored in queryable per-item/per-unit datasets after period source verification.

**Publication status:** Research ledger has anchor examples, but full REF-05 economic calibration remains an open research workstream; the 12 gaps above must be populated before the specialist document is marked final.
