### 4. Phase A — Real labor demand
Labor demand emerges from actual work. General Store needs labor for: customer service, receiving deliveries, stocking, measuring/wrapping, cleaning, bookkeeping, procurement, deliveries, management. Blacksmith: fabrication, repair, horseshoeing, receiving materials, fuel handling, customer interactions, maintenance. Farm: feeding, harvesting, planting, animal care, transport, equipment maintenance. **No hard-coded universal required headcount.** Use existing Tasks, WorkPackages, service queues, equipment, production methods, Person-time authority. Staffing depends on real workload, coverage, available labor hours, economics. One-person business can operate when one person can do the work. Excess work → queues, delays, missed sales, backlogs, overtime, lower service availability → legitimate hiring pressure.

### 5. Phase B — Contact-based recruitment
Recruitment through real information channels: personal relationships, referrals, existing employees, prior correspondence, local inquiries, handbills, advertisements, newspapers, messages, appropriate historical channels. **A candidate must not appear because the employer opens a hiring screen.** Recruitment costs legitimate money/information/attention/time. Messages can be delayed; recipients can ignore, refuse, negotiate. Preserve relationship provenance. Player must not automatically know every worker's exact skills. Use existing evidence/confidence rules from Canon XXXI. **No universal modern recruitment marketplace.**

### 6. Phase C — Real employment agreements
Hiring is negotiated: compensation, duties, schedule, start date, trial/probation, location, duration, housing/board, authority, conditions, termination. Recipient may accept, refuse, counter, clarify, delay, abandon. Employer must have legal/contractual authority to make the offer. Employment must NOT grant ownership or residential rights. Employer-provided accommodation needs separate/linked agreements. Family work, casual labor, apprenticeships, permanent wage employment stay distinct. **Do not turn all household work into conventional payroll.**

### 7. Phase D — Person employment decisions
People evaluate jobs against actual lives: current income, household needs, savings, existing job, expected wages, travel, conditions, skills, employer reliability, accommodations, relationships, safety, schedule, alternatives. Adequate resources → may refuse undesirable work. Unemployed with dependents and declining reserves → searches urgently. **NPCs must not auto-accept the highest wage regardless of commute, risk, family.** Workers can seek, negotiate, start, perform, change employers, resign, be dismissed, pause, seek other livelihoods. Decisions use only legitimately-known information.

### 8. Phase E — Scheduling and actual work
Connect employment to authoritative Person-time and Task systems. Scheduled employee performs actual work in available time. **No person simultaneously works two incompatible jobs, travels, shops, sleeps, or does another exclusive activity.** A job does not create labor hours. Managers assign tasks by required work, available people, capability, equipment, workplace capacity, duration, obligations, priorities. Person may do multiple compatible task families. **Occupation is descriptive, not an action-permission gate.** Skill affects effectiveness/mistakes/results without mechanically forbidding unfamiliar work. Refusal, fatigue, danger, contractual restrictions stay meaningful.

### 9. Phase F — Wages and household income
Connect to the household economy. On actual payroll event: employer money decreases, employee-controlled money increases, correct transaction records, liabilities/arrears if owed but unpaid, household resources change only per actual sharing arrangements. **Use the existing authoritative cash ledger. No second wage-payment pathway. No double-recorded wage expense. No wage income merely because status says Employed.** Wages reflect actual agreement, period, eligible work/guarantee. Test: ordinary payroll, partial periods, insufficient employer cash, unpaid wages, departure, family labor, multiple compatible jobs. Earning wages visibly lets a household purchase goods, meet needs, save, pay debts.

### 10. Phase G — Autonomous management
Implement Canon XXXI delegation model. Instruction semantics: **Do Once** (one specified action), **Manager Judgment** (ongoing objective with managerial discretion), **Owner Locked** (persistent prohibition/strategic boundary, not overridable without authorization). Managers are ordinary persons needing authority, time, information, capability, legitimate resource access. Within delegated authority they manage: staffing, scheduling, procurement, inventory policies, pricing within bounds, production, maintenance, routine customer service, ordinary collections, supplier relationships, shortage responses. Major strategic decisions may need approval per standing policy. **A manager cannot ignore the owner's max borrowing permission or sell prohibited assets.** No perfect foresight; judgment and information quality affect outcomes.

### 11. Manager activity and observability
Meaningful explainable records: e.g. hired clerk because queues grew; delayed purchase for insufficient cash; changed procurement after sales fell; rejected supplier over price; requested owner approval for a loan; refused order over equipment capacity. Expose: manager identity, business, observed conditions, decision, evidence, authority used, resulting task/agreement/transaction, outcome, exceptions/escalations. **No huge repetitive logs for minor actions.** Compact read models for later Unity UI.

### 12. Full owner autonomy
Prove a player-owned business can run an extended simulated period with minimal owner involvement: manager staffs ordinary work, responds to shortages, procures inventory, maintains operations, collects revenue, pays workers, escalates unusual problems, reports performance. **Not guaranteed profitability** — managers can decide poorly, hit shortages, fail. **No passive income bypassing ordinary transactions.** Portfolio coordination reuses/extends existing authority, no global player-only automation bonus.

### 13. Employment and migration (bounded)
Connect job opportunities to housing/household systems. Person may investigate distant job: travel, household circumstances, credible availability, wages, housing, moving costs, information, opportunity cost. Real decisions and movement. **Job posting must not auto-spawn a worker; vacant house must not auto-generate a resident.** Keep bounded — no complete new demographic engine. Secondary priority if it needs substantial unrelated refactoring.
