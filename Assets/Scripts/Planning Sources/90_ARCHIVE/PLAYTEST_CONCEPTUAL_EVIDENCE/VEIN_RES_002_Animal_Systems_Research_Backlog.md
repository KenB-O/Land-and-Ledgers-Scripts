# LAND & LEDGERS — Animal Systems Research Backlog
**Finding:** VEIN-RES-002 | **Status:** Historical research / technical implementation hold, not Canon-locked
**Coverage:** United States, 1870–1905; dated and regionalized, including northern Black Hills.

## Scope
A historically grounded sweep of consequential domestic, feral, wild, pest and game species. Document their real prevalence, economic roles, ecology and interactions, not just their visible models. Avoid assuming every species must have its own fully simulated individual.

### 1. Draft, pack and transport
Horses, mules, donkeys/burros, oxen, working cattle: historical regional/date availability; purchases, financing and sale; team configurations; harness/yokes; load and traction; roads and terrain; speed; forage/feed and water; hoof care and farriers; animal disease, fatigue, age, breeding and mortality; suitability to freight, stage, mining and agriculture. Mules are hybrids and generally infertile. Compare mixed-team feasibility and transport constraints, not a universal 'best animal' upgrade ladder.

### 2. Other domesticated animals
Beef and dairy cattle, pigs, sheep, goats, chickens, turkeys, geese, ducks, dogs, cats and other materially relevant species. Track real ownership and husbandry, breeding, products, labor, facilities, food, medical care, animal markets, wintering and losses. Guard, herding, hunting and pest-control animals have causal jobs and limits.

### 3. Regional wild fauna
Bison, pronghorn, deer, elk, moose, bears, wolves, coyotes, mountain lions, foxes, rabbits, hares, beavers, muskrats, squirrels, prairie dogs, pocket gophers, woodrats, ground squirrels, voles, chipmunks and native mice; plus birds, fish, reptiles, amphibians and insects where relevant. Track habitats, climate, hunting/trapping, predators, crop or livestock losses, ecosystem roles, population pressure and Indigenous resource relationships as appropriate for date/place.

### 4. Rodent deep dive
Questions: How prevalent were house mice, brown/Norway rats, roof rats and native rodents? Where and when did each species establish? How often did stores, grain warehouses, stables, cabins, homes, farms and mills suffer losses?

Potential mechanics: settlement/cargo introductions, food availability, building openings, seasonal breeding and local habitat; grain/flour/feed consumption and contamination; damage to sacks, wiring only where historically plausible, wooden fittings and inventory; burrowing and crop damage; livestock feed wastage; physical traps, cats, better storage, cleanliness and pest-control costs. Keep damage attached to actual lots and affected locations. Distinguish native beneficial species from pests and avoid uniform rodent density across the United States.

Chronology caution: CDC dates plague's introduction to the US to around 1900; never make 1877 Dakota plague endemic through an anachronistic rodent event.

### 5. Research deliverables
- Species × region × date prevalence evidence matrix, with 'unknown' where numeric densities are unsourced.
- Species × economic function matrix, including costs, benefits, hazards, predators and transport.
- Proposed minimal Canon/Technical mechanics and physical state authorities.
- 1870/1877/1880/1890/1905 comparisons and representative frontier vs established-town settings.
- Freight/traction comparative tests for horses, mules, donkeys and oxen, with route and load limits.
- Rodent tests for granary, general store, barn and residence, including loss and mitigation accounting.
- Audit Unity code: user reports horses appear implemented; mules, donkeys and oxen must be verified, not assumed implemented.

- South Dakota State history: Fort Pierre–Deadwood traffic used horse, mule and ox teams. https://news.sd.gov/news?id=news_kb_article_view&sys_id=d3f9c1c41bd469506e4aa97ae54bcb74
- Smithsonian on introduced house mice and brown rats: https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/chesreport/species_summary/mus%20musculus and https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/chesreport/species_summary/rattus%20norvegicus
