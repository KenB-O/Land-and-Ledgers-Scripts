# Historical verification notes — dated/legal constraints

**Release:** 2026-10-31 v3.0. These checks are extra sources, not a replacement for the full legal/historical specialist references. Do not transplant modern administrative requirements into the 1870–1905 simulation.

## R-001 — Black Hills territorial/treaty history
- Source: National Archives, Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868): https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/fort-laramie-treaty
- Source: National Park Service, President Ulysses S. Grant and Federal Indian Policy: https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/president-ulysses-s-grant-and-federal-indian-policy.htm
- Supported: the Black Hills were included in the Great Sioux Reservation under the 1868 treaty; miners' entry during the 1874–1876 rush was not automatically lawful; coercive dispossession and later legal disputes must not be flattened into 'vacant land'.
- Implementation: use dated and geocoded legal/land-status authority and preserve distinctions between a historically occurring act, legal validity and effective enforcement. Scenario fictional possession is not evidence that land was open.

## R-002 — Unpatented claim is not fee-simple surface ownership
- Source: Bureau of Land Management, About Mining and Minerals: https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/mining-and-minerals/about
- Supported: the 1872 General Mining Law concerns eligible mineral deposits on federal lands **open to mineral entry**; legal land status matters.
- Source: BLM, Important Information You Should Know (contemporary explanatory sheet): https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/documents/files/2017_Buying%20Mining%20Claim.pdf
- Historical limitation: BLM's modern recordation/maintenance standards and 1976+ registration practices MUST NOT be backdated into 1877. Use period statutes, Dakota territorial law, historical recording practices and specific treaty status for playable 1877 claim rights.

## R-003 — Territorial/state chronology
- Source: Library of Congress, Territories to Statehood: https://guides.loc.gov/chronicling-america-northern-west-territories
- Confirmed: North Dakota and South Dakota joined the Union November 2, 1889. Earlier actions require territorial rather than anachronistic state jurisdiction.
